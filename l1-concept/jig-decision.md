# Jig Decision

## Decision

Use purchased rated grips for specimen contact and reserve custom fabrication for adapters, alignment aids, and guard mounts.

## Option A: Purchased Rated Grips With Custom Adapters

Chosen path.

Pros:

- Known capacity from manufacturer documentation.
- Better jaw geometry, concentricity, and repeatability than ad hoc shop jaws.
- Custom work stays outside the highest-wear specimen contact surfaces.

Cons:

- Requires matching the machine nose/thread/pin interface.
- May need adapter drawings and lead time.

## Option B: Fully Custom Wedge Grips

Rejected for L1.

Pros:

- Could match unusual specimen ends.
- Useful as a future design study.

Cons:

- Highest safety burden.
- Jaw angle, hardness, serration, wedge retention, and anti-ejection details all become critical.
- Not appropriate without full drawings, calculations, and proof testing.

## Option C: Threaded or Button-Head Specimens

Candidate path for specific materials.

Pros:

- Positive axial retention can reduce jaw slip.
- Good fit for machined round specimens when shoulder geometry is controlled.

Cons:

- Threads or shoulders can become stress concentrators if specimen prep is poor.
- Requires careful alignment to avoid bending strain.

## Option D: Outsource the Test Fixture

Fallback path.

Pros:

- Best path when certified data or operator safety signoff is required.
- Vendor can match grips, adapters, shields, and load cell range as a system.

Cons:

- Less useful for documenting hands-on shop learning.
- Cost and lead time are higher.
