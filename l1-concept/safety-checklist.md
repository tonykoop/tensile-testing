# Safety Checklist

## Stored-Energy Hazard

Risk tag: `risk:safety`. Tensile testing intentionally stores elastic energy in the frame, specimen, grips, and adapters until fracture or slip releases it. The fixture must be designed as though something will fail suddenly.

## Stop-Work Gates

- Stop if any load-chain component has unknown rating, unknown material, damaged threads, or visible deformation.
- Stop if predicted peak load approaches the load-cell, grip, adapter, fastener, or frame limit.
- Stop if guarding is missing, loose, crazed, cracked, or not positioned between the operator and fracture zone.
- Stop if the specimen is visibly misaligned or bending under preload.
- Stop if anyone needs to stand in the fracture plane during a powered pull.

## Required Controls Before First Pull

- Machine owner approval.
- Current manufacturer manual for the test frame, controller, load cell, and grips.
- Shield or enclosure installed and inspected.
- Remote or shielded operator position identified.
- Eye protection and local shop PPE required.
- Emergency stop path known and unobstructed.

## Pre-Test Setup

- Estimate peak load from material strength and specimen area.
- Confirm the lowest-rated load-chain component controls the allowed test range.
- Confirm fixture fasteners are installed with the specified grade, length, engagement, and torque.
- Confirm extensometer or strain measurement hardware cannot become a projectile.
- Announce the pull and keep bystanders outside the hazard zone.

## After Failure

- Wait for motion to stop and load to return to zero before opening guards.
- Treat fractured specimen ends as sharp.
- Inspect jaws, adapters, pins, fasteners, and shield before the next pull.
- Record any slip, impact, deformation, or abnormal sound as a fixture issue, not just a test artifact.
